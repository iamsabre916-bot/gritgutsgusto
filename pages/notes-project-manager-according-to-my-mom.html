Project Manager: according to my mom, and in reality

12 March 2026 · Ir. Ts. Dr. Bibi Sabrena Sakandar Khan

My mom thinks my job is simple. "You're a Project Manager... so you manage the project."

If only it were that simple.

When you're delivering large-scale, safety-critical infrastructure in a brownfield environment, the role changes entirely.

Some days you're an operations manager, making sure the system will work smoothly once it goes live.

Other days, you're a risk manager, working ahead and planning for what might go wrong before it happens.

Then all of a sudden, you're a systems integrator, making sure civil works, electrical systems, signalling, the washplant, operations, consultants and contractors all speak the same language.

And in between all that, you're also:

  • managing stakeholders
  • protecting the project schedule
  • negotiating scope changes
  • translating technical issues for decision makers
  • facing internal and external audits
  • and sometimes even fighting scope creep

Working on a programme like the KLIA Aerotrain replacement, inside a fully operational international airport, really makes this sink in.

Brownfield projects do not give you a blank canvas. You're working around existing systems, ongoing operations, safety constraints, regulators, contractors and public expectations, all moving at the same time.

Some days it is like doing five puzzles at once, with the clock running.

But once the system is finally running, passengers are moving smoothly and operations have stabilised, it hits you: behind a simple title like "Project Manager" is a job that quietly holds an entire ecosystem together.

To everyone out there who works on complex infrastructure projects... I see you.
