Good Acopotot, Bad Acopotot: a career guide nobody asked for

11 November 2026 · Ir. Ts. Dr. Bibi Sabrena Sakandar Khan

Satire. Written by a professional cat joker. Any resemblance to real cats, real offices or real envelopes is purely coincidental. Please do not try this at home, at the office, or during a tender evaluation.

Meet two colleagues from the same office. Same age, same intake, same induction programme, same free lanyard.

Good Acopotot is honest, works hard, follows the process and puts the organisation first. He reads the procurement manual for fun. He asks for minutes of meetings. He has highlighted Clause 7.3 in four different colours.

Bad Acopotot wears a crown and sunglasses indoors. He has a gold chain with a dollar sign, a purple suit, and a desk stacked with cash that he calls "petty cash." Nobody has ever seen a petty cash claim form on that desk. Only petty cash.

Ten years later

Good Acopotot has debts, a family to support, work pressure that follows him home, and a seat in meetings where everyone suddenly checks their phone when he talks.

Bad Acopotot has money, a big position, a famous face at every event and a lifestyle that needs a second garage. For the second luxury car. And the third.

The annual appraisal

Good Acopotot

  • Technical competency: Excellent
  • Integrity: Excellent
  • Attitude: "Difficult to handle"
  • Comments: "Too rigid. Always asking for documents. Asked why the variation order was dated after the work was done. Not a team player."

Bad Acopotot

  • Technical competency: Not assessed (he was at a vendor's open house)
  • Integrity: Not applicable
  • Attitude: "Very flexible"
  • Comments: "Excellent stakeholder relationships. Very practical. Always says okay. Never offers a solution. Nods very well."

A short glossary of office language

"Can be flexible or not?" Can you ignore the rule I have not mentioned yet.

"Let's be practical." Let's skip the part where we check.

"Takkan itu pun tak boleh tolong?" I am about to ask for something you definitely cannot do.

"Kita kan satu team." The team is me and the vendor. You are the audit trail.

"Don't be so difficult." You said no, and you said it in writing. Three pages of writing, so long that the boss got dizzy and never finished reading it.

"Let's take this offline." Let's discuss this somewhere without minutes.

How to tell you have been labelled difficult

You stop getting invited to lunch, but you still get invited to the meeting where they need someone to blame.

You type in the WhatsApp group and three people leave "seen."

Your proposal is rejected for "lack of consultation," and the consultation meeting is cancelled because you are "too detail."

The vendor knows about the decision before you do.

So Good Acopotot had an idea

One night, Good Acopotot sat at his desk, adjusted his spectacles, looked at his bank statement, looked at Bad Acopotot's Instagram, and asked one simple question: how do I get RM50 million in one go?

He made a list.

Conduct a bank heist. Very cinematic. Unfortunately, he cannot run fast (too much fat), he gets breathless climbing stairs, and he will probably stop to check whether the vault door has a valid maintenance certificate.

Sell sensitive information during a tender bid. Bad Acopotot always seems to know the tender results early. Or the specifications somehow fit only one vendor in the whole country, down to the colour of the bolts. (It happens!) Maybe there is a special course. Good Acopotot checked. It is not HRD Corp claimable.

Take a loan from an ah long. Instant approval, no documents, no credit check. The collection team does home visits and leaves friendly notes in red paint. Sometimes they even repaint your house before Eid. Very customer-centric.

Ask a vendor for money to approve "something." Nobody ever says what the "something" is. It is the most flexible word in the procurement dictionary.

Any other ideas? Asking for a friend. The friend is a cat.

Integrity, it turns out, does not pay the car loan. Being corrupt it is. Time to sell the integrity. Condition: slightly used, one careful owner.

Good Acopotot's first day as Bad Acopotot

He put on his "Ray-Ban" sunglasses from Eco-Shop. He walked into the vendor meeting. The vendor slid a red envelope across the table.

Good Acopotot opened his laptop and said, "Okay, before we proceed, can I have this in writing? Email is fine. With your company stamp, the date, and the reference number of the approval?"

The vendor took the envelope back. The meeting ended early. Good Acopotot was marked "difficult to handle" by both sides. His boss called him a failure. Even at corruption.

Some cats just cannot be corrupted properly. It is a defect in the factory setting.

The fine print Bad Acopotot did not mention

The crown is rented. The cash on the desk is evidence. The friends at every event are the same people who will tell the investigators, "I barely knew him, we only met at the vendor's open house. Twelve times."

WhatsApp messages last longer than the money.

And Good Acopotot? Still tired. Still paying debts. Still the one they call when the project is in trouble and somebody needs to actually fix it. But he sleeps at night, he can look his kids in the eye, and nobody is knocking on his door at 6 a.m. with a search warrant and a camera crew.

So korang rasa, what is the best way to get RM50 million?

Still waiting for a good answer. Until then, this Acopotot stays good. Slow, broke, difficult to handle, and proud of it. Insya Allah.

Meet the real Acopotot

The cartoon cats are made up. The real Acopotot is not.

Acopotot is the name I gave my pet squirrels and my black-and-white cat. None of them has ever been offered an envelope.
