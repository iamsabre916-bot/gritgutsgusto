Flagship programme

Grit, Guts & Gusto: Manoeuvring Crisis in Project Management

Duration2 days (14 hours), 9:00 am to 5:00 pm
Group size15 to 25 participants
BringA laptop

Every project will hit a crisis at some point. A critical test fails, a key vendor walks away, a client or regulator says no, or the budget runs out. On high-profile projects, add politics and public criticism to the list. Most project recovery courses focus on the paperwork. This programme focuses on the person who has to lead through it.

Participants practise basic project controls, risk tools, decision trees, critical thinking and stakeholder management using real cases from airport and rail projects, with exercises they apply to their own work. The programme ends with a live crisis simulation.

Who it is for

Middle to top management, project and programme managers, project directors, heads of department and senior specialists who lead projects or respond to project escalations.

By the end, participants will be able to

  1. Triage a troubled project using measurable warning signs (milestone slippage, ageing issues, rework, scope changes against budget reserve) and recommend rescue, re-plan or stop.
  2. Build a decision tree and other business tools to justify one crisis decision.
  3. Use AI to red-team a decision and apply the Ask, Challenge, Decide, Own checklist.
  4. Deliver a two-minute Board briefing and a holding statement.
  5. Produce a one-page recovery charter and a personal Crisis Playbook.

Methods

Short lectures, real case studies, group exercises with decision trees and other analysis tools, a bias-spotting activity, hands-on AI labs, stakeholder briefing practice and a live crisis simulation with debrief.

Strategic Thinking: Stop Firefighting, Start Foresight

Duration1 day (7 hours), 9:00 am to 5:00 pm
Group size15 to 30 participants
BringA laptop

Most managers get promoted for solving today's problems. Then they are expected to shape tomorrow. In reality, most of the week goes to firefighting and the strategy stays on the shelf.

This programme gives leaders a simple way to step back, look at the bigger picture and make decisions that will still make sense in three years. Participants practise five tools (PESTLE, TOWS, the 2x2 priority grid, scenario planning and the one-page strategy map) on their own work. Examples come from real strategy roadmaps and business cases. AI is used to test scenarios and challenge assumptions, but the leader still makes the call.

Who it is for

Managers, heads of department, senior specialists, team leads and high-potential executives preparing for senior roles.

By the end, participants will be able to

  1. Reframe one daily problem at operational, tactical and strategic levels.
  2. Complete a PESTLE scan and TOWS analysis for their own unit.
  3. Select three priorities and one activity to stop using a 2x2 grid.
  4. Test their priorities against three AI-generated scenarios.
  5. Build a one-page strategy map and deliver a two-minute strategic pitch.

Methods

Short lectures, a personal time audit, real workplace case studies, group workshops using PESTLE, TOWS and the 2x2 grid, an AI scenario lab, strategy map building and a two-minute pitch to a mock management committee.

Conflict Management: Leading Stakeholders & Difficult Conversations

Duration1 day (7 hours), 9:00 am to 5:00 pm. Half-day (4 hours) version available
Group size15 to 25 participants
BringA laptop or phone

Many projects fail because of poor communication, low stakeholder engagement and conflict left unresolved, rather than technical failure. Most of the time, the argument on the table is not the real issue. It is about trust, roles and pressure.

Managers today also deal with new kinds of friction: staff worried that AI will change their roles, arguments that start in WhatsApp groups, three generations working in one team, and disputes with suppliers or service providers. Using discussion, games and realistic workplace scenarios, participants learn to keep their team focused, manage stakeholders and turn difficult conversations into agreed actions.

Who it is for

Project managers, supervisors, executives, specialists, coordinators and technical staff moving into leadership or client-facing roles, and managers of teams or suppliers.

By the end, participants will be able to

  1. Apply 3 practical techniques to keep a project team focused under pressure.
  2. Map stakeholders on the Power/Interest Grid and plan how to engage each one.
  3. Choose and justify a conflict style, and negotiate an agreed outcome in a supplier dispute exercise.
  4. Conduct a difficult conversation using the OPEN framework, rehearsed with AI and a peer.
  5. Produce a personal improvement action plan.

Methods

Short lectures, a card-matching game, a guided stakeholder case study, a conflict style self-check, the Four Corners class game, OPEN framework role plays, a negotiation exercise, rotating role plays and an AI rehearsal lab.

Duration2 days (14 hours)
ForExecutives, engineers, operations and support teams

Pick one manual process, digitalise it in small steps and show results the business can see. Participants pick one small digital win, simplify the process first, and build a working tracker or dashboard with free tools.

At Malaysia Airports, Sabrena led the move to an electronic document management system and an award-winning dashboard for quality-of-service monitoring. She has two papers on digitalisation in airport project delivery accepted at WiSET 2026.
